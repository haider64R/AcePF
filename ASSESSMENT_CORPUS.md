# Supplied FAST PF assessment corpus

This is a **curated, evidence-bound sample** of the 14 PDFs supplied for the September 2026 corpus task. It is not a claim about every FAST PF paper or a complete transcription. The PDF inventory and observed assessment profiles live in `src/content/assessment-sources.js`; imported questions live in `src/content/assessment-corpus.js` and are appended to the existing `questions` bank. The PDFs remain the source of truth. Nothing here changes the C++ execution engine or the UI.

## Sources and provenance

The inventory records 10 distinct exam PDFs, 3 practice PDFs, and 1 byte-identical duplicate of the Spring 2024 solution PDF. The 10 exams comprise 3 Sessional 1, 3 Sessional 2, 2 Final, and 2 Midterm papers. `PF Sessional#1 (Fall-2024).pdf` contains only printed pages 1 and 4 in two PDF pages; `PF Final Exam (Fall-2023).pdf` contains 17 image-only pages although its cover says 18. The latter was inspected as page images for broad structure but was not transcribed into question records. The Fall 2024 scan supports one imported open coding question from printed page 4. The 2020 Sessional II solution has no visible cover metadata beyond its document name and Question 01; campus, paper total and duration remain unknown. `PF-Sessional1-Questions.pdf` lacks a reliable exam cover, so its questions are classified as **practice** even though the filename mentions Sessional 1.

Every imported item has a stable ID, document filename, PDF page number, question/part label, a human-readable reference, canonical topic IDs, difficulty, answer/explanation, auto-grading decision, Visualizer compatibility, and verification status. Exam records use `source.type: "past-paper"`; genuine supplied practice uses `"practice"`; original built-in questions remain `"authored"`. Do not relabel practice as an administered exam. Do not infer missing years or marks from filenames alone. The schema permits unknown year and marks while still requiring a page-checkable source. An item appearing in two distinct papers uses one record with `source.references`; the two Spring 2024 solution files are byte-for-byte identical and are not counted twice.

Formatting of imported snippets was normalized where needed (spacing, typographic quotes, and wrapping snippets with `main` for deterministic execution). The underlying expressions, control flow, values, and errors were preserved. These normalized executable snippets are explicitly identified by their source reference; consult the PDF before using a question as a facsimile of the printed paper. Long design questions are stored as concise faithful prompts rather than claiming a unique code answer.

## What these papers actually show

Sessional 1 is varied: the two Fall 2022 papers contain expression values, logic construction, output/error snippets, code completion and a larger conditional program, with different totals (30 and 60 marks). The partial Fall 2024 scan shows short traces and later open coding/bitwise tasks on its visible pages; its missing middle pages prevent a full distribution estimate. The Fall 2018 and Fall 2020 Midterm papers also mix short traces with longer coding, rather than following one fixed format.

Sessional 2 increases state tracking. The two complete Fall 2022 papers put 35/60 (BS-CS) and 36/60 (BS DS/AI) marks into tracing or output/error work, but their remaining tasks differ: the CS paper includes array checkpoints and completion, whereas the DS/AI paper has three 8-mark programs. The Fall 2020 supplied solution is only a seven-snippet Question 01 excerpt. Repeated traps include integer/floating conversion at function calls, nested calls, for/do-while update order, switch fall-through, shadowed loop variables, array updates, and prefix/postfix state.

The Spring 2024 Final combines one-line pointer/function statements, many output/error parts, and longer bitwise, pattern, range and 2D-array programs. Its cover declares 100 marks in three hours, but the six printed question headers add to **102**; both figures are retained rather than silently reconciled. The Fall 2023 cover declares 170 marks in three hours. Its page images show pointer, array, and longer code tasks, but the scan quality and missing printed page make question-level counts unreliable. The structured paper evidence in `assessment-sources.js` preserves these differences instead of asserting an institution-wide template.

Practice material is separate. The FIDS workshop emphasizes diagnostic dry runs, deliberate bugs, infinite loops and undefined behavior; its difficulty can exceed short exam snippets. The datatype sheet tests integer/floating division, widths, overflow and unsigned behavior. `PF-Sessional1-Questions.pdf` mixes literals, output/error work, code completion and a flowchart, but cannot be established as an exam from its pages. These are useful candidates for later Challenges only after product-level selection; source classification must stay practice.

## Verification and limitations

`verification: "execution-verified"` means the normalized snippet's answer/state was checked against native C++17 and the unchanged Visualizer. `"answer-reviewed"` means a source answer or C++17 reasoning was checked, but the snippet may not run in the Visualizer. `"source-only"` denotes an open design task without a unique verified solution. `"manual-review"` keeps an ambiguous question available for editorial review without presenting it as automatically gradable. `autoGradable` is independent of `visualizer.compatible`: two deterministic questions have a native-checked answer but use syntax the current Visualizer does not support. A one-line or open coding answer is not auto-graded merely because the source solution contains one example.

Known source issues were preserved and marked. The Fall 2018 `++x * ++x` / `x++ * ++x` question and workshop `--n * n++` have no portable C++17 output because of unsequenced modifications. The datatype sheet's signed `int` overflow likewise has no portable output despite the sheet's stated sizes. An AIDS Fall 2022 ticket-booking task overlaps age discount brackets at 5 and 15, so it awaits manual boundary review. The Spring 2024 final solution contains some questionable or nonportable sample answers; imported executable questions were checked independently rather than trusting all printed solutions. The Fall 2023 scan, missing Fall 2024 pages, some interleaved solution annotations, and layout-fragmented snippets were omitted from the verified executable subset. Omission is intentional, not evidence that those papers lack the topics.

## Adding another real paper

1. Inspect every PDF page, including rendered images for scanned or diagram-heavy pages. Record only visible metadata in `assessmentSources`; explicitly record gaps and duplicate files.
2. Add a question to `assessmentQuestions` with its exact PDF filename, one-based PDF page, printed question/part label, marks only if visible, canonical topics, and an answer/explanation supported by the source or verification. Keep code and intentional errors faithful. Use `source.references` for an exact repeated question.
3. Mark open, ambiguous, implementation-dependent, undefined, and unsupported code appropriately. Do not change the interpreter to accommodate a paper. Give `autoGradable: true` only to a deterministic answer with a safe comparison contract; `visualizer.compatible: true` only after a successful unchanged-engine run.
4. Update the inventory/profile evidence as needed and add tests for provenance, selection, answer verification, and any newly discovered ambiguity. Run the complete suite and the existing 35 native comparison programs.

Future V1 should query this single bank by source, assessment, scope, type, verification and compatibility. It can use observed gaps to author new **clearly authored** FAST-style practice, select vetted short questions for Challenges, display original sourced questions in Past Papers, compose evidence-based Mock Exams, and show non-auto-gradable tasks in Exam Mode without claiming to score them automatically.
